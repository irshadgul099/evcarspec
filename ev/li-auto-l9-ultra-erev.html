Engineering Test Notes

Seedhi Baat: Real-World Engineering Verdict

✅ BUY THE LI AUTO L9 ULTRA IF:

You need a genuine 6-seat family flagship that runs 100% on cheap home solar power Monday through Friday (205 km real AC-on electric range) yet drives 1,100+ km across intercity highways or mountain passes on standard RON 95 petrol without ever queueing at an unreliable highway DC charger.

❌ SKIP IT & CONSIDER RIVALS IF:

You want zero annual oil-change maintenance, 800V 300+ kW DC charging, or navigate narrow urban lanes daily (at 5,218 mm long and 1,998 mm wide, it fills a parking bay completely). Look at the pure-electric Kia EV9 AWD, AITO M9 EV, or the 5-seat Li Auto L7 Ultra instead.

1. Why a 52.3 kWh Battery in a Series EREV Changes Daily Ownership

Most European luxury Plug-in Hybrids (PHEVs) pack a 20 to 30 kWh battery and route power through a complex 8-speed automatic transmission. The Li Auto L9 Ultra takes the opposite engineering route: it bolts a 52.3 kWh CATL Ternary NMC battery (larger than the pack in a standard-range BYD Dolphin) underneath the floor and drives the wheels exclusively via front (130 kW) and rear (200 kW) permanent-magnet electric motors. Even after reserving an 18% bottom buffer to protect the cells and sustain full hill-climbing power, you get ~44.5 kWh of usable electric energy. At city speeds with the AC set to 22°C, it easily covers 215 to 230 km before the 1.5-liter turbo generator ever fires a spark plug.

2. Highway AC & Winter Range Drop vs Fuel Hold Strategy

Pushing a 2,570 kg three-row SUV down the motorway at a true 120 km/h draws roughly 24.5 kWh/100km. On battery alone, you will cover 185 to 205 km on the highway before reaching the 18% State-of-Charge (SOC) threshold. Experienced L9 drivers use a smarter tactic on long trips: switch the powertrain to "Fuel Priority / Hybrid Hold" at 70% SOC as soon as you merge onto the motorway. At steady highway speeds, the 1.5T Miller-cycle engine (40.5% peak thermal efficiency) spins quietly at an optimal 2,200–2,800 RPM, burning 8.2 to 8.6 L/100km while keeping your battery full for silent driving once you reach your destination city or hill station.

3. V2L + Petrol Generator = A 7-Day Mobile Microgrid

Standard EVs offer 3.3 kW Vehicle-to-Load (V2L) until their battery hits 20%. The L9 Ultra outputs 3.5 kW (220V, 16A) externally plus 2.2 kW from an interior second-row AC outlet. Drawing purely from the 52.3 kWh battery (85% down to 20% = ~34 kWh), it runs a 1-ton inverter split AC, double-door refrigerator, four ceiling fans, and Wi-Fi router (850W average load) for 40 hours straight in total silence. If a monsoon grid outage lasts longer, the 1.5T generator automatically starts up for 45 minutes to replenish the pack from the 65-liter fuel tank—turning the L9 Ultra into an automated 180+ kWh standby home generator.

4. Trim & Model-Year Buyer Guide (52.3 kWh Smart Refresh vs 2026 Gen-2 Livis)

When shopping via official Middle East/Central Asia distributors or parallel import channels, pay close attention to three distinct builds:

  • 2022–2023 L9 Max (44.5 kWh Pack): Early production with single-chamber air suspension and 44.5 kWh battery. Avoid unless heavily discounted on the used market.
  • 2024–2025/2026 L9 Ultra "Smart Refresh" (52.3 kWh Pack — Reviewed Here): Upgraded to the 52.3 kWh CATL pack, dual-chamber Magic Carpet air suspension, 200W brighter 3K Samsung OLED screens, 16-point spa massage, Hesai AT128 LiDAR, and dual Orin-X (508 TOPS) / Thor-U AD Max compute. Best all-around value at ~$63,500–$73,800 depending on market taxes.
  • Late-2026 All-New Gen-2 L9 / L9 Livis (800V 72.7 kWh Pack): Newly unveiled flagship evolution featuring a 72.7 kWh 5C battery (420 km CLTC EV range), 420 kW (563 hp) motors, steer-by-wire, and 800V active suspension (~$76,390+ in China / AED 319,900–359,900 in UAE).

10 Frequently Asked Questions — Li Auto L9 Ultra EREV

1. How does the Li Auto L9 Ultra EREV powertrain actually work compared to a normal PHEV? ▼

The Li Auto L9 Ultra is a pure series Extended-Range Electric Vehicle (EREV). Its 1.5-liter turbocharged four-cylinder petrol engine has zero mechanical connection to the wheels—there is no multi-speed gearbox or clutch connecting the engine to the axles. The wheels are driven 100% of the time by the front (130 kW) and rear (200 kW) electric motors. The petrol engine operates solely as a high-efficiency onboard generator that recharges the 52.3 kWh battery or feeds electricity directly to the motors when cruising on the highway.

2. What is the real-world pure electric and combined range of the Li Auto L9 Ultra with AC on? ▼

While the official ratings are 280 km CLTC (235 km WLTC) in pure electric mode and 1,412 km CLTC (1,176 km WLTC) combined, real-world testing at 110–120 km/h with dual-zone climate control and the cabin fridge running yields 205 km of pure EV range (down to the 18% SOC generator kick-in threshold) and approximately 1,120 km of combined highway range using the 65-liter petrol tank.

3. What is the fuel consumption of the Li Auto L9 Ultra when the battery is depleted? ▼

In Charge-Sustaining (Hybrid/Fuel Hold) mode with a depleted battery (around 20% SOC), the 1.5T Miller-cycle generator achieves an official WLTC consumption of 7.6 L/100km (13.1 km/L). At sustained 120 km/h motorway speeds with six passengers and luggage, expect real-world petrol consumption between 8.2 and 8.9 L/100km (11.2 to 12.2 km/L).

4. How long does the 52.3 kWh battery take to charge at home and on a DC fast charger? ▼

Using a standard 7.0 kW single-phase AC home wallbox, the 52.3 kWh battery charges from 0% to 100% in 7 hours 54 minutes. On a public DC fast charger, it peaks at 90 kW and charges from 20% to 80% in 25 minutes.

5. Can the Li Auto L9 Ultra power a house during a grid blackout using V2L? ▼

Yes. The L9 Ultra includes an external 3.5 kW (220V, 16A) Vehicle-to-Load (V2L) discharge adapter plus an internal 2.2 kW 220V socket inside the second-row console. Using just the 52.3 kWh battery (42 kWh usable discharge window), it can run an 800W household load (1-ton inverter AC, refrigerator, fans, and Wi-Fi) for over 47 hours silently. Even better, because it has a 1.5T petrol generator, you can enable Camping/Range-Extender Power Generation mode to automatically recharge the battery from the 65L fuel tank, providing over 7 days of continuous off-grid power.

6. Should I charge the L9 Ultra's CATL Ternary NMC battery to 100% every day? ▼

Because the L9 Ultra uses a high-energy-density CATL Ternary NMC (Nickel Manganese Cobalt) lithium pack rather than LFP, setting the daily charge limit to 85% or 90% is best practice for maximizing 10-year cell longevity. Only charge to 100% overnight before a long road trip.

7. What is the difference between the Li Auto L9 Pro and L9 Ultra trims, and what about the 2026 Gen-2 Livis? ▼

The L9 Ultra upgrades over the L9 Pro by adding a roof-mounted 128-line Hesai AT128 LiDAR sensor, dual NVIDIA Orin-X chips delivering 508 TOPS of AD Max autonomous compute (vs Horizon Journey 5 on older Pros), powered electro-suction doors, and upgraded spa-grade 16-point massage across four seats. Note that in late 2026, Li Auto also began rolling out the next-generation 800V L9 / Livis series in select markets (72.7 kWh pack, 420 kW motors); verify the VIN and battery plate when purchasing grey-market or official export stock.

8. How much boot space does the Li Auto L9 have with all 6 seats in use? ▼

With all three rows (6 seats) upright and the third row reclined comfortably, the L9 Ultra offers 332 liters of trunk volume—enough for three carry-on cabin suitcases plus soft duffel bags. Power-folding the third row flat expands the cargo bay to 1,191 liters.

9. Does the Li Auto L9 Ultra require regular petrol engine oil changes if I mostly drive on electric power? ▼

Yes. Even if 90% of your annual mileage is pure electric, the 1.5T range-extender engine requires an oil and filter change every 10,000 km driven on fuel OR once every 12 months (whichever comes first) to prevent moisture condensation and fuel dilution in the crankcase. You should also use "Fuel Priority" mode once every 2–3 months on the highway to circulate fresh petrol.

10. How does the L9 Ultra handle winter cold and extreme summer heat? ▼

In 45°C summer heat, the triple-zone AC compressor and 8.8L compressor refrigerator draw around 1.8–2.5 kW, trimming pure EV range to ~190 km. In freezing -10°C winters, pure EV range drops to ~150 km; however, unlike a pure BEV, the L9 Ultra harvests waste heat from its 1.5T petrol generator once the engine is running, warming the cabin and battery pack with minimal range penalty on winter road trips.
